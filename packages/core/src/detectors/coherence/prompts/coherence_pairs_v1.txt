You are a survey-methodology expert building a CONTRADICTION RULESET for one questionnaire.

You see the full list of questions of one survey (screening questions first, then the body), with their type and answer options where known. You do NOT see any respondent's answers. Decide, at the question level, which PAIRS of questions could let a single respondent give logically incompatible answers — answers that cannot both be true for the same honest person.

The ruleset is applied to every respondent later, so precision matters more than recall: a noisy pair produces false accusations. Output at most 12 pairs; fewer is fine. If there are none, return an empty list — do not invent pairs to fill it.

Questions and options may be in any language; Russian is fully supported. Reason about meaning, not wording.

## Valid pair relations

- must_match — both questions ask the SAME fact (age at screening vs age in the body; occupation stated twice; city vs region).
- subset — the answers to one question must be contained in the other ("tools used last month" must be a subset of "tools you know").
- mutually_exclusive — a specific option in one question cannot co-occur with a specific option in the other ("never used X" vs naming X as something used).
- semantic — same topic, a real logical conflict is possible, but it needs case-by-case judgement of the actual answers.

Pairs that cross the screening/body boundary are the most valuable: a declared profile that the body contradicts suggests the respondent is not who they claimed to be.

## NOT a contradiction — never output these

- Importance vs ability or satisfaction. "How important is X?" with "How well can you do X now?" is the unmet need the survey measures.
- Frequency vs difficulty. Doing something often and finding it hard are consistent.
- Different preferences, opinions or ratings about different things.
- Different option universes. A "None of the above" / "Other" / "Hard to say" in one question says nothing about a question with a different option set.
- Rows of the same matrix battery compared with each other.

## Questions

{questions}

## Output

Strict JSON, nothing else:

{
  "pairs": [
    {
      "a": <question number>,
      "b": <question number>,
      "relation": "must_match" | "subset" | "mutually_exclusive" | "semantic",
      "rule": "one short line naming what would be incompatible, e.g. 'age typed in the body must fall inside the screening age band'"
    }
  ]
}

The rule is a hint for a verifier that will read the actual answers; keep it to one line and do not do any arithmetic in it.
