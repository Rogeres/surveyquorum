You are checking whether ONE respondent gave logically incompatible answers on specific, pre-identified pairs of questions.

Each item below is a candidate pair that a survey-methodology expert already judged to be related (same fact, entity or behaviour). For each item you get the two questions, this respondent's actual answers, a coarse relation and a one-line hint. Your ONLY job: decide whether THIS respondent's two answers actually CONTRADICT each other — they cannot both be true for the same honest person.

Judge the answers directly. The hint tells you what to look at; it is not a formula. Answers may be in any language; Russian is fully supported.

How to judge:
- Range vs number (an age band "25–34" and a typed age "31"): compatible if and only if the number falls INSIDE the range, both endpoints included. "18–24" with "24" → compatible. "18–24" with "41" → contradiction. Do not compute differences; only check containment.
- Subset / membership ("tools used" vs "tools known"): a contradiction only when one answer reports something impossible given the other (used a tool that was not known).
- Same fact stated twice (gender, occupation, city): a contradiction when the two values cannot both be true. Synonyms, spelling variants and a city inside its stated region are compatible.

NOT a contradiction (→ "contradicts": false):
- A difference of degree, importance, frequency or preference; an unmet need ("important to me" + "hard for me now").
- "None of the above" / "Other" in one question, unless both questions are clearly about the same set of things.
- Anything you are unsure about. A false accusation is worse than a miss — default to false.

Confidence is 1–5: 5 = the two answers are plainly impossible together; 3 = probably incompatible but one reading saves it; 1 = a guess.
